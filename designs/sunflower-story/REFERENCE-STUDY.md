## Visual findings

Typography has several distinct roles: printed comic captions, lettering on physical surfaces, and large lettering composed into scene space. A single full-screen title animation would miss this variety.

The sampled frames use both warm daylight and deep cool night scenes. Pink brick, amber light, purple/blue shadows and relatively dark foreground silhouettes matter as much as bright accents. Uniform cyan/magenta lighting would not match these samples.

The scenery is dimensional and richly authored. Perspective, characters, camera framing, surface detail, lighting and selective print texture work together. A generic city silhouette plus a screen-wide halftone overlay would not reproduce the inspected compositions.

## Proposed portfolio adaptation — not observations from the video

Build a cinematic sequence with four chapters: introduction, selected work, approach, contact. Present short original portfolio phrases in staggered captions, on walls or street surfaces, and at scene scale. Use Steven Joe Valdez's verified identity and the existing Ayos! facts; leave unknown contributions and technical details explicitly pending.

Make scroll the default timeline so visitors control the pace and can revisit a phrase. Provide direct chapter navigation and a stable readable project/details view. Optional autoplay should be pausable. Reduced motion should present the same content in still compositions.

An exact visual match requires detailed scene artwork or modeled assets, character animation and shot-specific typography. Browser layout and procedural geometry alone can prototype the staging and interactions, but cannot honestly be presented as an exact recreation of the film's rendered imagery.
